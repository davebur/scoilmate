# ScoilMate: security, privacy and safeguarding
Design requirements, not completed controls or legal advice. No real student data in this planning repository.

## Permissions and authentication
Parent reads and manages only students linked to them via `parent_student` (add, settings, deactivate, reports); cannot link themselves to an existing student, see other families, or see admin pages. Student reads only their own history/progress; may request eligible published lessons/quizzes and submit own open items. Admin manages accounts/content; exports/erasure require explicit reason, recent reauthentication and audit. No student enumeration. Default deny; every object lookup scoped to current principal. Test student A against student B's attempt IDs, URLs, exports and hidden fields. UI hiding is not authorisation.

Use opaque 256-bit random server-session cookie tokens; store token digests, not bearer values. Cookie `__Host-scoilmate_session`, Secure, HttpOnly, SameSite=Lax, Path=/, no Domain in HTTPS production. Separate explicit development configuration; never deploy insecure cookie defaults. Rotate on login/privilege change, revoke on logout/password change/disable. Proposed expiry: students 60 min idle/12 h absolute; admins 20 min/8 h, no persistent remember-me for pilot. Review usability on shared school machines. No tokens in URLs/localStorage. Private pages Cache-Control:no-store.

Password hashes: Argon2id through maintained library, unique library-generated salts and encoded parameters; benchmark cost above OWASP baseline (19 MiB, t=2, p=1 at time checked). Rehash on login when policy changes. No reversible/plaintext storage, security questions or silent truncation. Permit long passphrases, reasonable upper bound to prevent DoS. Invite/recovery tokens random, hashed, expiring, single-use; admin-assisted recovery must verify identity through an agreed trusted channel. No shared/default admin credential; MFA required before any public admin exposure, choose maintained implementation later.

CSRF synchronizer tokens bound to server sessions for all state-changing requests including login/logout, answers, publishing and deletion. Validate Origin/Referer and Fetch Metadata as defence in depth; SameSite and CORS are not substitutes. GET never mutates. Use same-origin by default, no wildcard credentialed CORS.

Rate-limit by account plus IP (school NAT considered), not permanent account lockouts. Initial tuning proposal: 5 failed logins/account/15 min with capped backoff; 30/IP/15 min; recovery 3/account/hour; submit 60/student/min and quiz creation 20/hour. Return 429/Retry-After, generic login/recovery messages, clear counters with approved expiry. Limits require load/usability testing; one-process memory-only counters lose state on restart, nginx alone cannot enforce account rules.

Template autoescaping, plain text/minimal sanitised authoring, CSP without unsafe script, no remote embeds/CDNs by default; test stored XSS in admin content. Parameterised ORM queries; body/file limits; no uploads in MVP. Host allowlist, trusted-proxy list only for loopback/nginx, TLS, safe redirects, minimal error pages, secrets outside Git, restrictive DB permissions, dependency/security review and redacted logs. Separate answer-key models: no keys/explanations in pre-submission HTML, JSON, JS or source maps. They are revealed after submit; open practice is not tamper-proof examination security.

## Children, Ireland and GDPR
Most intended learners are minors. Determine who is controller and any processors before onboarding. Family, public direct-to-student and school-managed use have different responsibilities; “educational” is not a lawful basis. Record purpose, necessity, Article 6 basis, data flow, recipients, transfers, security, retention and rights handling. School public task may be relevant in the appropriate context but is not automatically available to a private operator. Do not switch to legitimate interests simply to evade consent; assess children's rights and actual circumstances. Obtain qualified advice where necessary.

**Irish digital age of consent is 16 specifically where Article 8 applies:** consent-based processing in relation to information society services offered directly to a child. It is not a blanket ban on under-16 access and does not mean every school activity requires parental GDPR consent. If relying on consent in that setting for under-16s, obtain/verify parental authorisation with proportionate reasonable efforts; document withdrawal and avoid collecting excessive ID. Another lawful basis must genuinely apply, not be assumed. School permission or a parent's signup does not by itself settle the analysis. Follow DPC child-oriented guidance and complete DPIA screening; a full DPIA is required where likely high risk, assess before any pilot involving monitoring/profiling children.

Minimum data: random ID, alias/login, password hash, role/status, selected subject/track/year, learning events. No full DOB, PPSN, home address, school name, photos, disability/health data or child email by default. If consent/age assurance is necessary, design minimal proportionate evidence rather than collecting birth certificates. Provide short age-appropriate notice plus detailed adult notice, contact, access/correction/export/erasure workflow, and complaint route. Parents do not automatically have unrestricted access to all children's records; assess identity, rights and best interests.

Proposed retention for approval: closed-pilot learning records until pilot end plus 90 days; inactive accounts reviewed after 12 months; security logs 30 days; expired sessions/tokens promptly purged; encrypted backups rotating 30 days. These are product proposals, not legal retention requirements. Erasure includes caches, exports and expiry of backups, with deletion replay on restore. Production data never goes to development Git, analytics or external AI tools. Assess processor contracts, EEA locations and transfer safeguards if external services are later chosen. Breach process includes containment, investigation, risk assessment, DPC notification within 72 hours where required and affected-person notification where high risk.

## Safeguarding
No chat/contact discovery, public profiles, rankings, ads, manipulative streaks or student uploads. Adults manage content; reviewed age-appropriate historical material may need contextual notices. Feedback avoids humiliation. Provide a route to report inaccurate/upsetting content without soliciting sensitive disclosures. Define designated adult contact, triage and escalation policy; do not promise 24/7 monitoring or confidential counselling. Decide applicability of Irish safeguarding obligations to the eventual operator/service with competent advice. Child-data controls and safeguarding review block release, not a post-launch enhancement.
