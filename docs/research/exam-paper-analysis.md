# Junior Cycle exam paper analysis: English, Gaeilge, History (2022–2026)

Purpose: show what the SEC Junior Cycle final exams actually ask, so ScoilMate's topics and activity types build towards it.
Prepared: 2026-11-14. Method: downloaded all papers below from the SEC archive, extracted the text with `pdftotext`, then reviewed the structure, question stems and the 2025 marking schemes (English HL, History, Gaeilge T2 HL).
Copyright: this document summarises structure, question types and skills. It does not reproduce exam questions or passages. Where it names a topic, text or speaker from a paper, that is only so the topic can be identified.

## 2. Subject analysis

### 2.1 English (Higher and Ordinary)

**Fixed format (all years, both levels):** 2 hours (09:30–11:30), **180 marks**, single answer booklet. Every question is compulsory, with no choice between questions. Each paper has an announced **theme**, and sections are named by skill. The section names and marks change every year, so students cannot learn a fixed layout.

| Year | HL theme | HL sections (marks) | OL theme | OL sections (marks) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2022 | Hope | Writing to engage 40 · Figurative language 70 · Reading images in film 30 · Responding to texts 40 | A World of Wonder | Communicating & responding 45 · Imagining & creating 65 · Reflecting on reading 70 |
| 2023 | The Art of Storytelling | Characters & film 40 · Narrative in poetry 50 · Appreciating story 55 · Shaping stories & emotions 35 | People and Places | Reading & creating 60 · Responding imaginatively 75 · Analysing & creating 45 |
| 2024 | Interesting Interactions | Appreciating character 75 · Responding to literary texts 45 · Critical appreciation 60 | Journeys and Adventure | Reading comprehension 60 · Writing for a variety of purposes 50 · Responding imaginatively 70 |
| 2025 | Perception | Appreciating language 45 · Writing to explain & comment 40 · Character, setting & story 40 · Listening & writing with imaginative purpose 55 | Different Viewpoints | Analysing information 30 · Exploring language & ideas 60 · Engaging with reading 60 · Communicating as a writer 30 |
| 2026 | Critical Reflection | Writing to evaluate 35 · Character, setting & story 40 · Shakespearean drama 50 · Writing to analyse & imagine 55 | Interesting Worlds | Visual texts 35 · Writing for a variety of purposes 60 · Exploring & using language 40 · Analysing & creating 45 |

**Question counts:**

- HL has 9–10 questions worth 5–35 marks each. Typical values are 15–25 marks.
- OL has 10–14 questions worth 5–40 marks each, with more 10–15 mark items.

**Marking (2025 HL scheme):**

- Examiners use holistic "General Indicators of Quality": **Focus, Ideas, Development, Coherence, Expression, Register, Creativity, and Spelling and punctuation**.
- Responses are placed in High, Middle or Low bands.
- Spelling and punctuation can cost at most about 10% of a question's marks, for example 0–3 marks on a 30–35 mark question.
- This list maps directly to a self-assessment rubric (see §4).

**Skills tested (English):**

- Finding information and making inferences.
- Explaining a writer's choices, with reference to the text (quotation or close reference).
- Comparing viewpoints, and evaluating with a justified opinion.
- Writing for a given audience, purpose and register in a set format.
- Imaginative writing, including continuing, imitating style or changing perspective.
- Applying studied texts to an unfamiliar prompt that is linked to the theme. This is the decisive skill at HL: students must re-angle prepared knowledge, because no question can be pre-learned.

### 2.2 History (Common level)

**Fixed format:**

- 2 hours, **360 marks**, **8 compulsory questions** of about 45 marks each.
- Each question is built around one or more **sources**: text extracts, photographs, paintings, woodcuts, posters, maps, timelines, tables, graphs, census returns or advertisements. Each question has 4–8 sub-parts.
- The paper is published in English and Irish versions.

**Marking (2025 scheme):**

- Mostly **3 marks per valid point**, sometimes capped (for example 12 or 18 marks in total).
- Some questions need a specific answer. Others accept any valid point ("etc.").
- **Spelling and grammar are not penalised.**
- The scheme says that Strand 1 learning outcomes (the nature of history) are built into questions across the whole paper.

**Pattern:**

- Every paper samples across all three strands: nature of history, Irish history, and Europe and the wider world.
- Roughly 3 questions are on earlier periods (to about 1700) and 5 on modern history.
- Many questions end with an open part such as "name the X you studied and describe…". These favour students who have prepared one detailed case study for each learning-outcome area, for example one reformer, one Renaissance figure, one conquistador, one revolution, one world war, one dictatorship, one cultural movement and one theme of change.

#### Recurring sub-question types (all 5 papers unless stated)

1. **Retrieval from a source** ("according to Source 1…"), worth about 3 marks each. This is the largest share of marks.
2. **Primary or secondary source with a reason** (5/5).
3. **Chronology**: how many decades or years a timeline covers, drawing arrows from events to dates, ordering developments 1–7, building a timeline from a biography (2022, 2023, 2024, 2026).
4. **Matching key terms to explanations or captions**, or filling gaps from a word list. Examples include artefact, Reformation, diaspora, genocide, propaganda and Cold War terms (5/5).
5. **Cause and consequence**: name or explain causes, consequences or impact. There is a dedicated item classifying statements as cause or consequence (2025).
6. **Opinion with evidence**: "in your opinion… give two reasons using the source", positive or negative impact, "is it fair to…" (2023–2026).
7. **Comparison**: similarities and differences between two images, posters or periods; continuity and change (5/5).
8. **Usefulness and reliability of a type of evidence**: graphs, songs, album covers, emigrant letters, maps versus text, drone photos, archives versus libraries.
9. **Perspective and bias**: whether an account is positive, negative or neutral; differing views of Columbus; the message a cartoonist intended; propaganda.
10. **Short "account of" or description** of a studied person, event or development, with bullet-point prompts and about 4–6 points (5/5).
11. **Historical significance** ("why is this considered significant", "which change was most significant").
12. **Historian's method**: which sources you would use, what you'd put in an exhibition, what an archive is.

### 2.3 Gaeilge (T1 and T2, Higher and Ordinary)

**Fixed format (all four papers, every year 2022–2026):**

- 2 hours (09:30–11:30), **270 marks**.
- Roinn A is **Cluastuiscint** (listening), with 3 questions:
  - HL: 45 marks.
  - OL: 60 marks.
  - Item types, each heard twice: a **píosa cainte** (short talk), a **fógra** (announcement) and a **comhrá** (conversation, split into 2 parts).
- Roinn B is Léamh, Ceapadóireacht agus Litríocht (reading, writing and literature):
  - HL: 225 marks.
  - OL: 210 marks.
- The question order and marks were **identical in all five years** within each paper. This makes the papers very predictable, which is ideal for revision scaffolding.

#### Gaeilge T1 Gnáthleibhéal (Irish T1, JC083G)

- Listening: 60 marks.
- Q4: poster comprehension, 20 marks.
- Q5 and Q7: literature (dán, úrscéal/dráma, or a film poster in 2026), 25–35 marks.
- Q6: notice, 15 marks.
- Q8: **léamhthuiscint**, 65 marks.
- Q9: **gramadach**, 20 marks.
- Q10: **ceapadóireacht**, 30 marks: blag (2022, 2025) or *alt* (2023, 2024, 2026) on familiar topics such as phone, friends, favourite place, hobby and school life.

**Marking (2025 T2 HL scheme, writing):**

- One holistic mark per piece, using Ard/Meán/Íseal bands (50–44 / 43–25 / 24–5).
- Graded on:
  - focus and original ideas;
  - **how many of the 6 bullet points are developed** (5–6 developed for Ard, about 3 for Meán, 1 for Íseal);
  - register;
  - fluency and organisation;
  - accuracy in **verb tenses, séimhiú, urú, indirect speech, prepositions and noun declensions**.
- Implication for ScoilMate: **covering every bullet** and **verb-tense accuracy** are the cheapest marks to teach.

**Skills tested (Gaeilge):**

- Listening for specific detail (numbers, times, places, two-item lists).
- Paragraph-targeted reading retrieval.
- True/false.
- Personal opinion with reasons.
- Structured writing to bullet points in a given form.
- Grammar in context: cloze and forced choice.
- Literature responses on character, theme, feelings, setting and favourite moment, written as a review.
- T1 adds punctuation, dictionary literacy and more argumentative forms (aiste, moladh, ríomhphost to an authority).

**Recurring vocabulary themes (both tracks):** music festivals and concerts; famous Irish people (sport, music, film); the Gaeltacht and Irish-language culture (Oireachtas, TG4, Irish in media and TikTok); school life; friends and hobbies; holidays and travel; weather and storms; climate change and environment; technology and phones; food and health; special events and birthdays; shopping and the city.

## 4. Gaps: what MCQs cannot cover, and how to approach it

| Gap | Why MCQ is insufficient | Suggested approach |
|---|---|---|
| **Extended writing** (English: 20–35 mark responses; Gaeilge ceapadóireacht: 50 marks; History "account of") | Marks depend on Focus, Development, Coherence, Register and Expression (English), or on bullet coverage plus grammar accuracy (Gaeilge). Recognition is not production. | (1) **Writing frameworks** per form: required features, register and layout. (2) **Plan-first activity**: the student fills a plan (point, evidence, explanation ×3), and the app checks completeness. (3) **Model-answer comparison**: show an original High-band model *after* the attempt, with annotations. (4) **Self-assessment checklists** built from the SEC criteria: English (Focus, Ideas, Development, Coherence, Expression, Register, Creativity, spelling and punctuation); Gaeilge (all 6 bullets developed? tenses consistent? séimhiú/urú after common triggers?); History (one point = one specific, developed fact; aim for 4–6). |
| **Applying studied texts to unseen themes** (English, Gaeilge literature) | The prompt angle is unpredictable, so prepared answers alone fail. | A "text bank" where students store their own key moments and quotations per studied text, then **random theme-prompt drills** (for example "hope", "interactions", "perception") that require choosing and re-angling evidence in a 5-minute plan. |
| **Timing** | Every paper is 2 hours with many compulsory questions. English and Gaeilge give no choice, so time management decides the outcome. | **Timed plans**: 3–5 minute planning sprints, with marks-per-minute guidance (about 1.5 marks/min in English; about 2.25 marks/min in Gaeilge; about 3 marks/min in History, or about 15 minutes per question). Include mock section timers. |
| **Listening / cluastuiscint** (Gaeilge: 45–60 of 270 marks) | Needs audio. | Later phase: original, educator-reviewed recordings matching the three formats (píosa cainte, fógra, comhrá) with tick-box and short-answer questions. Do not redistribute SEC audio without permission. |
| **Oral communication** (English and Gaeilge CBAs; school-based, not on the paper) | Needs speech. | Out of scope for the MVP. Later: speaking prompts with a self-recording and checklist (no automatic grading promised). |
| **Visual and source analysis with images** | Needs rights-cleared images. | Use public-domain or out-of-copyright sources (paintings, archive photos with open licences, census returns) with metadata. Prefer original diagrams and timelines. |
| **Grammar production in Irish** (not just recognition) | Cloze MCQ trains recognition, but writing marks need production. | Transformation drills (rewrite in past or future tense, apply séimhiú after *mo/do/a*), typed short answers checked against accepted forms, with educator-reviewed answer keys. |

## 5. Priority topics for the first content batch

### English (3–5)

1. **Poetic and language techniques plus unseen-poem response.** Flashcards for terms, then a PQE short-answer response on an original short poem (HL and OL; tested every year).
2. **Functional writing forms**: diary, speech or talk, email or letter, review, blog or social post. Provide feature checklists and a structured writing plan for each form (every year, 2–3 items per paper).
3. **Unseen non-fiction comprehension**: retrieval, inference, vocabulary in context and writer's viewpoint, on original texts (OL every year; HL most years).
4. **Studied-text key-moment bank** (novel and film): character development, relationship and setting, plus theme re-angling drills.
5. **Shakespeare (HL)**: key scenes, suspense, resolution and themes still relevant today. Start with generic skills; play-specific content waits for the teacher's choice of play.

### History (3–5)

1. **Working with sources**: primary vs secondary, retrieval, usefulness, bias and perspective (Strand 1; on every question, every year).
2. **Chronology and key terms**: timeline skills plus a core glossary (every year).
3. **Archaeology and archives**: methods, dating and the role of archives and census records (4/5 years).
4. **Great Famine and emigration** (3/5) together with **the Holocaust** (3/5). Both appear as source-based questions on individual Irish experiences.
5. **Age of Exploration and the Renaissance** (3/5 and 2/5), including a case-study "account of" plan template (conquistador, Renaissance figure).

### Gaeilge (3–5)

1. **Question words and paragraph-based léamhthuiscint**, with true/false. Uses original short articles on festivals, sport and music, and famous people (all four papers, every year).
2. **Grammar in context**: verb tenses (past, present, future, conditional), the copula, séimhiú and urú after common triggers, and prepositions, in cloze and three-option formats (every year).
3. **Ceapadóireacht to bullet points**: blag and alt (T2 HL/T1), teachtaireacht plus cuireadh (T2 OL), with a phrase bank and a bullet-coverage checklist (every year; highest-value single question).
4. **Themed vocabulary**: music and festivals, sport, school, holidays and weather, environment and technology (every year).
5. **Sraith pictiúr / past-tense narration** (T2 OL every year, 35 marks), then **literature-response templates** once the cohort's prescribed text lists are verified (circular 0086/2024 for exams 2027–2029).

### Caveats

- Frequencies come from the 5 papers per level in 2022–2026, read through text extraction. Image-only content (posters, cartoons) was inferred from the question wording.
- Exam themes, section names and question angles in English change every year. Never present ScoilMate content as predicting specific questions.
- All ScoilMate passages and items must be newly written. Use SEC papers only for format and skill alignment, and link students to the official SEC archive for real past papers.
