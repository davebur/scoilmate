Exam stress and wellbeing

Feeling stressed before exams is normal, and you are not alone. A bit of stress can help you focus; too much can affect your sleep, appetite and concentration. Here are some things that help, and people you can talk to.

Things that help

  • Short, regular practice beats long cramming. About 20–25 minutes, then a break.
  • Sleep helps memory. Stop studying well before bed; nothing here is worth staying up for.
  • Move a bit every day: a walk, a kick-about, anything.
  • Talk to someone: a parent, a friend, a teacher, or your school's guidance counsellor.
  • Mistakes in practice are useful. They show you what to revisit, and nobody else sees them.

Official advice

Wellbeing support for exam students, parents and school staff (gov.ie, Department of Education): tips for students and parents, and a list of supports.

Free, confidential support

  • Jigsaw: mental health support for young people aged 12–25 and their parents, online and in person.
  • spunout: Ireland's youth information and support site. Their Text About It service: free-text SPUNOUT or HELLO to 50808, any time, day or night.
  • Childline (ISPCC): freephone 1800 666 666, any time, for anyone under 18.
  • Samaritans: freephone 116 123, 24 hours a day.

If you or someone else is in danger right now, call 112 or 999.
